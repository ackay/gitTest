Understanding Roof Pitch and Slope

Roof pitch is one of the most fundamental measurements in roofing. It determines which materials can be used, how quickly water drains, how much surface area must be covered, and how safely a crew can work on the roof. Getting the pitch right — and understanding what it means — is essential for every roofing project.

What is Roof Pitch?

Roof pitch is expressed as a ratio of vertical rise to horizontal run, typically written as X:12. This means for every 12 inches of horizontal distance (the run), the roof rises X inches vertically. For example, a 6:12 pitch rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal run — a moderately steep roof.

The "12" in the ratio refers to a standard 12-inch run, which makes it easy to compare pitches. A 4:12 roof is less steep than a 6:12 roof, which is less steep than a 9:12 roof. Pitch can also be expressed as a percentage (rise ÷ run × 100) or as an angle in degrees using arctangent.

Roof Pitch Categories

The roofing industry classifies pitches into general categories that guide material selection and installation methods:

  • Low slope (1:12 to 3:12, under 14°): Requires special low-slope roofing systems — built-up roofing (BUR), modified bitumen, TPO, EPDM, or metal. Standard asphalt shingles cannot be used below 2:12 per most manufacturer specifications.
  • Standard slope (4:12 to 5:12, 18–23°): The most common residential roof pitch range. Compatible with virtually all roofing materials including asphalt shingles, metal, wood shakes, and clay tile.
  • Moderate slope (6:12 to 8:12, 27–34°): Good drainage, most materials work well. Installers can typically walk with normal precautions.
  • Steep slope (9:12 and above, 37°+): Requires special steep-slope installation techniques, additional fasteners, and safety equipment. Some materials (like heavy clay or concrete tile) may not be suitable due to structural loading concerns.

Roofing Squares Explained

Roofing materials are typically sold and measured in roofing squares. One roofing square equals 100 square feet of roof surface area. A bundle of asphalt shingles covers approximately 33 square feet, so three bundles make one square. To calculate material needs, you first need the actual roof surface area — which is the footprint area multiplied by the slope multiplier (a number derived from the pitch that accounts for the sloped distance being longer than the horizontal distance).

For example, a 6:12 pitch has a slope multiplier of approximately 1.118 — meaning the actual roof surface is 11.8% larger than the flat footprint. A 12:12 pitch has a slope multiplier of 1.414, meaning the surface area is 41.4% larger than the footprint.

Material Compatibility by Pitch

Not every roofing material works at every pitch. Here's a quick reference:

  • Asphalt shingles: Minimum 2:12 with special underlayment; standard installation from 4:12 up
  • Metal roofing (standing seam): Works from 1:12 and up — excellent for low-slope applications
  • Metal roofing (exposed fastener / corrugated): Typically requires 3:12 minimum
  • Wood shakes and shingles: Minimum 4:12; best performance at 6:12 and steeper
  • Clay and concrete tile: Minimum 4:12; heavy — structural loading must be verified
  • Slate: Minimum 6:12 for most products; very heavy — engineered structural support required
  • TPO / EPDM / BUR: Ideal for flat and low-slope roofs (0:12 to 3:12)

Why Pitch Affects Shingle Quantity

When ordering shingles, you must account for the actual roof surface area, not just the building footprint. The slope multiplier converts the horizontal area to the sloped surface area. Additionally, steeper slopes require more nails and fasteners per shingle (to resist wind uplift), may need wider starter strips, and require additional safety equipment that can affect labor costs significantly.
