Everything You Need to Know About Sonotube Footings

Sonotubes are one of the most widely used products in residential construction. Originally a brand name from Sonoco Products, "sonotube" is now used generically to describe any cylindrical cardboard concrete form tube. Understanding how to size, place, and fill them correctly ensures your deck, fence, or structure will remain stable for decades.

What is a Sonotube?

A sonotube is a cylindrical tube form made from heavy-duty spirally wound paperboard. They are placed in dug holes, filled with fresh concrete, and typically peeled away after the concrete has cured (48–72 hours under normal conditions). The cardboard form creates a smooth round concrete column or pier that can be left exposed above grade for an attractive finish.

Common applications include:

  • Deck footings — The most common residential use; supports deck posts and beams
  • Fence post bases — Provides a solid anchor for wooden or metal fence posts
  • Mailbox and sign posts — Creates a permanent, frost-resistant base
  • Structural column bases — Supports pergolas, covered patios, and light structures
  • Light pole bases — Commercial and residential exterior lighting

Sonotube Volume Formula

Because sonotubes are round, their volume is calculated using the formula for a cylinder:

Volume (cu ft) = π × r² × h

Where r is the radius in feet (diameter ÷ 2 ÷ 12) and h is the height (depth) in feet. For example, a 12-inch diameter sonotube that is 4 feet deep: r = 12 ÷ 2 ÷ 12 = 0.5 ft, so Volume = 3.14159 × 0.5² × 4 = 3.14 cubic feet per tube.

Common Sonotube Diameters and Applications

  • 6-inch diameter — Very light loads; fence posts, mailbox posts, decorative columns
  • 8-inch diameter — Light deck posts (4×4), pergola posts, single-story deck corners
  • 10-inch diameter — Standard deck footings for 4×4 or 6×6 posts; typical single-story residential decks
  • 12-inch diameter — Heavier loads; 6×6 posts, two-story decks, larger spans
  • 16-inch diameter — Heavy beams, structural columns, engineered applications
  • 18–24-inch diameter — Commercial and engineered structural applications

Frost Line Depth: How Deep to Pour

The most critical aspect of sonotube footing design is frost line depth. Footings that do not extend below the frost line will heave as the ground freezes and thaws, eventually shifting and damaging whatever structure they support. Frost line depths by region:

  • Southern states (FL, TX Gulf Coast, LA) — 0–12 inches
  • Mid-South (GA, AL, MS, AR, TN) — 12–18 inches
  • Mid-Atlantic (VA, NC, KY, MO) — 18–24 inches
  • Northern states (PA, OH, IL, IN) — 24–36 inches
  • Deep north (MN, WI, ME, MI UP) — 42–60 inches
  • Canada — 48 inches or more depending on province

Always verify the exact frost depth requirement with your local building department. Most jurisdictions require a permit and inspection for deck footings.

Concrete Tips for Sonotube Pours

Use a minimum of 3,000 PSI concrete for sonotube footings. For footings in wet or freeze-thaw environments, use air-entrained concrete for improved freeze-thaw durability. Pour concrete in lifts and use a rod or vibrator to eliminate voids. If embedding a post anchor bolt or post base hardware, set it while the concrete is still wet and double-check alignment before it cures.

Frequently Asked Questions

A sonotube is a cylindrical cardboard tube form used to create round concrete footings, piers, and columns. The tube is placed in a dug hole, filled with concrete, and peeled away after the concrete cures. Sonotubes are most commonly used for deck footings, fence post bases, mailbox posts, pergola columns, and light pole bases. They create a clean, round concrete pillar that can be exposed above grade or buried below it.
The right diameter depends on the load the footing must support. As a general rule: use a 10-inch tube for 4×4 posts supporting light-to-moderate deck loads; use a 12-inch tube for 6×6 posts or sections carrying more weight; use a 16-inch tube for heavy beam loads or corner posts on large decks. Always consult your local building department — most jurisdictions have specific footing size requirements based on the tributary area each post supports, calculated from a soil bearing capacity chart.
Sonotube footings must extend below the frost line for your area to prevent frost heave from shifting the footing and damaging your structure. Frost line depths range from near zero in South Florida to 60+ inches in northern Minnesota and Canada. Check with your local building department for the exact required depth — it is also typically listed in the International Residential Code (IRC) frost depth maps. Many jurisdictions require footings to be at least 12 inches in diameter and extend 12 inches into undisturbed soil below the frost line.
A 12-inch diameter sonotube at 4 feet deep requires approximately 3.14 cubic feet of concrete (π × 0.5² × 4 = 3.14 cu ft per tube). That equals 0.116 cubic yards. In bags, you would need about 6 bags of 80 lb concrete (3.14 ÷ 0.60 = 5.2 bags, rounded up with overage) or about 8 bags of 60 lb concrete. Use this calculator to find the exact amount for your specific diameter, depth, and quantity of tubes.
Concrete should not be placed when air temperatures are below 40°F (4°C) without cold-weather protection measures. Concrete that freezes before it develops sufficient strength (about 500 PSI, typically 24–48 hours in normal conditions) suffers permanent, irreversible strength loss. In cold weather, use warm mix water, avoid cold aggregate, use a concrete accelerator additive, and protect the freshly poured footing with insulated blankets. Avoid pouring if temperatures will drop below freezing within 48 hours. In extreme cold, temporary heated enclosures may be required.
