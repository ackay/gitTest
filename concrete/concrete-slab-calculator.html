How to Calculate Concrete for a Slab

Calculating the right amount of concrete for a slab project is straightforward once you understand the formula. Concrete is sold by the cubic yard, so all dimensions must be converted to feet before calculating.

The Concrete Volume Formula

The standard formula for a rectangular slab is:

Cubic Yards = (Length × Width × Thickness in feet) ÷ 27

Since slab thickness is most commonly measured in inches, convert it to feet first by dividing by 12. For example, a 4-inch slab = 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet. Then multiply length × width × 0.333 and divide by 27 to get cubic yards.

Worked Example: 12 × 20 Foot Driveway Slab

For a 12 ft × 20 ft driveway at 4 inches thick:

  • Thickness in feet: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
  • Cubic feet: 12 × 20 × 0.333 = 80 cu ft
  • Cubic yards: 80 ÷ 27 = 2.96 cu yd
  • Add 10% overage: 2.96 × 1.10 = 3.26 cu yd — order 3.5 cu yd
  • 80 lb bags needed: 80 ÷ 0.60 = 134 bags (ready-mix is strongly recommended for this volume)

Bag Coverage Reference

When mixing concrete from bags, the yield per bag depends on the bag weight:

  • 60 lb bag — yields approximately 0.45 cubic feet
  • 80 lb bag — yields approximately 0.60 cubic feet
  • 40 lb bag — yields approximately 0.30 cubic feet (patch/repair use)

To find bags needed from cubic feet: divide total cubic feet by the bag yield. For cubic yards: multiply cubic yards by 27 first to get cubic feet, then divide by bag yield.

Standard Slab Thicknesses by Application

  • Driveways (passenger cars) — 4 inches minimum; 5 inches for added durability
  • Driveways (heavy vehicles, RVs) — 5–6 inches minimum
  • Sidewalks and walkways — 4 inches standard
  • Residential patios — 3.5 to 4 inches
  • Garage floors — 4 inches minimum; 5–6 inches recommended for vehicle parking
  • Shed bases — 3.5 to 4 inches

Concrete Ordering Tips

When placing a ready-mix order, always specify the PSI strength (3,000 PSI is standard for most residential slabs), the slump (4-inch slump is workable for most pours), and any additives like air entrainment for freeze-thaw climates or fiber reinforcement. Confirm truck access to your site before scheduling the pour.

Most ready-mix suppliers have a minimum order of 1 cubic yard and charge a short-load fee for smaller quantities. For jobs under 1 cubic yard, bagged concrete is often the more practical and economical choice.

Reinforcement Options

Concrete is strong in compression but cracks under tension. Reinforce slabs based on application:

  • Rebar (#3 at 18" centers) — Best for driveways, garage floors, and any slab subject to vehicle loads
  • Welded Wire Mesh (6×6 W1.4) — Common for residential patios and light-duty slabs
  • Fiber Mesh — Added directly to the mix; reduces plastic shrinkage cracking; good for patios and walkways

Control joints should be cut or tooled every 8–12 feet in each direction to prevent random cracking as the concrete shrinks during curing.

Frequently Asked Questions

A 10 × 10 foot slab at 4 inches thick requires approximately 1.23 cubic yards of concrete (10 × 10 × 0.333 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cu yd). Add a 10% overage buffer and order about 1.35 cubic yards. At this volume, bagged concrete is a practical option — you would need about 56 bags of 80 lb concrete or about 74 bags of 60 lb concrete.
Each 80 lb bag of pre-mixed concrete yields approximately 0.60 cubic feet. Since one cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet, you need approximately 45 bags (27 ÷ 0.60 = 45) of 80 lb concrete to fill one cubic yard. For 60 lb bags (0.45 cu ft each), you need about 60 bags per cubic yard.
The right slab thickness depends on the intended use. 4 inches is the standard for sidewalks, patios, and residential driveways with normal passenger car traffic. 5–6 inches is recommended for driveways that will support heavy vehicles, RVs, or trucks. Garage floors are typically 4–6 inches depending on load. Always check local building codes, as some jurisdictions have specific requirements for permitted concrete work.
For slabs requiring more than about 1 cubic yard (roughly a 10×10 at 4" thick), ordering ready-mix concrete delivered by truck is strongly recommended. Mixing that many bags by hand is extremely labor-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to finish consistently before the first batches begin to set. Ready-mix also guarantees a uniform, specified-strength mix throughout the pour. Save the bagged concrete for small projects, repairs, and post footings.
PSI (pounds per square inch) measures the compressive strength of cured concrete after 28 days. For most residential applications, 3,000 PSI is the standard specification — suitable for driveways, sidewalks, patios, and garage floors. Use 3,500–4,000 PSI for slabs that will carry heavy vehicles, for footings, or when the local climate subjects the concrete to frequent freeze-thaw cycles. Higher PSI concrete costs slightly more but provides increased durability and longevity.
