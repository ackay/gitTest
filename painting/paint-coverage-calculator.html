How to Calculate How Much Paint You Need

Getting the paint quantity right saves money, prevents mismatched color batches, and avoids the frustration of running out halfway through a project. The calculation involves finding your total paintable wall area, accounting for doors and windows, multiplying by the number of coats, and dividing by the paint's coverage rate.

The Paint Coverage Formula

Here's the step-by-step calculation our calculator performs:

  • Gross Wall Area = Wall Length × Wall Height × Number of Walls
  • Door Deduction = Number of Doors × 21 sq ft (standard 3×7 door)
  • Window Deduction = Number of Windows × 15 sq ft (standard 3×5 window)
  • Net Wall Area = Gross Wall Area − Door Deduction − Window Deduction
  • Total Paint Area = Net Wall Area × Number of Coats
  • Gallons Needed = Total Paint Area ÷ Coverage Rate (sq ft/gallon)

The recommended purchase quantity rounds up to the nearest half-gallon to ensure you don't run short, while keeping over-purchase to a minimum.

Paint Coverage Rates by Surface Type

The coverage rate you enter in our calculator has a major impact on the final gallon estimate. Using the right coverage rate for your specific surface ensures an accurate result:

  • Smooth, previously painted walls: 380–400 sq ft/gallon
  • Light orange peel texture: 300–350 sq ft/gallon
  • Medium orange peel or knock-down: 250–300 sq ft/gallon
  • Heavy texture (popcorn, heavy knock-down): 200–250 sq ft/gallon
  • Bare new drywall (without primer): 200–300 sq ft/gallon
  • Bare wood or masonry: 200–300 sq ft/gallon (use primer first)

Paint Types for Interior Walls

Choosing the right paint type and finish is as important as calculating the right quantity. Modern interior paints fall into several categories based on their binder chemistry and sheen level:

  • Flat/Matte: Zero to minimal sheen. Excellent for hiding surface imperfections and providing a non-reflective look. Less washable than sheen finishes — best for ceilings, low-traffic bedrooms, and formal spaces.
  • Eggshell: Low, soft sheen. The most popular interior wall finish. Provides good washability without the glare of satin. Ideal for living rooms, dining rooms, and master bedrooms.
  • Satin: Medium sheen, velvety appearance. Very washable and scrubbable. Best for high-traffic areas, kids' rooms, hallways, and family rooms.
  • Semi-Gloss: High sheen, very durable. Standard for kitchens, bathrooms, and all trim, doors, and cabinetry due to its moisture resistance and cleanability.
  • Gloss: Very high sheen, maximum durability. Used for furniture, accent trim, and cabinets where maximum hardness and washability are needed.

Tips for Buying Paint Efficiently

Professional painters know a few tricks to buy the right amount without waste:

  • Buy in 5-gallon buckets when you need more than 3 gallons of the same color — it's typically 15–20% cheaper per gallon than individual quarts or gallons.
  • Check batch numbers if buying multiple cans of the same color. Even from the same store, different batches can have slight color variations. Box (mix) all cans together in a large bucket before starting.
  • Buy an extra quart for touch-up work and store it with the batch number noted on the lid. Touch-up paint needs to be applied carefully to avoid lap marks on flat finishes.
  • Check return policies before buying. Many stores accept unused, unopened cans within 30 days. This reduces the risk of buying a little extra as a buffer.

How Surface Preparation Affects Paint Performance

Even the best paint calculator can't compensate for poor surface preparation. Proper prep is the foundation of a quality paint job:

  • Clean the walls — Remove grease, dirt, and dust with a trisodium phosphate (TSP) solution or mild detergent. Paint over dirty walls will peel prematurely.
  • Fill holes and cracks — Use lightweight spackle for small holes and joint compound for larger repairs. Sand smooth when dry and spot-prime before painting.
  • Sand glossy surfaces — Lightly sand or degloss existing glossy paint to improve adhesion for the new coat.
  • Tape and protect — Mask trim, outlets, and switch plates. Use drop cloths on floors and furniture. Quality masking makes the difference between a professional and amateur result.

Paint Coverage Calculator — Frequently Asked Questions

A standard 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has approximately 384 gross square feet of wall area (4 walls × 12 ft × 8 ft). Subtracting one standard door (21 sq ft) and one window (15 sq ft) gives about 348 sq ft of net wall area. With two coats at 400 sq ft/gallon coverage, you need approximately 1.74 gallons — purchase 2 gallons. For textured walls or a dramatic color change, buy 2.5–3 gallons to be safe.
A gallon of standard interior latex paint covers approximately 350–400 square feet per coat on smooth, previously painted walls. On textured walls (orange peel, knock-down), coverage drops to 250–300 sq ft per gallon. On bare drywall or new construction without primer, plan for 200–300 sq ft per gallon. Always check the specific coverage rate on the paint can label, as premium paints may cover more than budget paints.
You need primer in the following situations: painting over bare drywall or bare wood; covering stains, water damage, or smoke damage; making a dramatic color change (especially going from dark to light); painting over a glossy surface; or painting over porous surfaces like concrete or masonry. For repainting over existing paint in a similar color on sound walls, primer is generally not necessary. Using primer reduces the number of finish coats needed, improving coverage and saving money on finish paint.
Most rooms require two coats of paint for even, thorough coverage and a professional finish. One coat may be sufficient when repainting with the same or a very similar color over an existing sound finish. Three or more coats are sometimes needed when making a dramatic color change (particularly covering a dark color with a lighter one), painting with naturally low-opacity colors like red, orange, or yellow, or painting over patched areas and drywall repairs that remain visible after the first coat.
Choose your interior paint finish based on the room's traffic level and function: Use flat or matte for low-traffic rooms and ceilings — it hides surface imperfections beautifully. Use eggshell for living rooms and bedrooms — a slight sheen that's washable and forgiving. Use satin for high-traffic areas, hallways, children's rooms, and family rooms — durable and easy to clean. Use semi-gloss for kitchens, bathrooms, and all trim and doors — very washable and moisture-resistant. All modern interior latex paints are low-VOC or zero-VOC, making them much safer than older oil-based paints for occupied living spaces.
