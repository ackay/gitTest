How Much Paint Do You Really Need?

Buying the right amount of paint is more nuanced than it might seem. Too little and you're making an emergency trip to the store — potentially getting paint from a different batch with a slightly different color. Too much and you're paying for cans that end up in the garage for years. Professional painters use precise calculations to buy exactly what they need, with a small buffer for touch-ups.

Paint Coverage Rates Explained

Every can of paint lists a coverage rate — how many square feet one gallon covers in a single coat. Standard interior paints typically cover 350–400 square feet per gallon on smooth, previously painted walls. However, several factors affect actual coverage:

  • Texture: Textured walls (orange peel, knock-down, popcorn) have more surface area and absorb more paint. Expect 200–300 sq ft per gallon on heavily textured surfaces.
  • Color change: Going from a dark color to a light one (or vice versa) may require three or more coats. Deep, saturated colors (reds, oranges) are notoriously difficult to cover and may need extra coats.
  • Surface porosity: New drywall, bare wood, and previously unpainted surfaces absorb significantly more paint than finished walls. Always prime first on new surfaces.
  • Application method: A brush or roller applies paint differently than a sprayer. Sprayers tend to apply thinner coats, potentially reducing effective coverage per gallon.

Choosing the Right Paint Finish

Paint sheen (finish) affects both the look of the finished surface and its durability and cleanability. Matching the right finish to the room type is as important as choosing the right color:

  • Flat / Matte: No sheen, hides imperfections well, but difficult to clean. Best for low-traffic areas: ceilings, formal dining rooms, master bedrooms.
  • Eggshell: Slight sheen, washable, very forgiving. The most popular finish for living rooms and bedrooms.
  • Satin: Moderate sheen, very washable, slight glare. Ideal for high-traffic areas: hallways, family rooms, children's rooms.
  • Semi-Gloss: Higher sheen, very durable, easy to clean. Standard for kitchens, bathrooms, trim, and doors.
  • Gloss: Very high sheen, maximum durability and washability. Used for furniture, trim, cabinets, and accent elements.

When to Use Primer

Primer is not just for new construction. You should use primer when:

  • Painting over bare drywall or wood (new construction or repairs)
  • Making a dramatic color change — especially going from dark to light
  • Covering stains, water damage, or smoke damage
  • Painting over a glossy surface (use a bonding primer)
  • Painting over a porous surface like bare concrete or masonry

Using a quality primer reduces the number of finish coats needed, saving both time and paint cost. For most dramatic color changes, one coat of primer plus two coats of finish paint outperforms three or four coats of finish paint alone.

Calculating Paint for Multiple Rooms

When painting multiple rooms, calculate each room separately, especially if the ceiling heights or room dimensions differ. Then add the quantities together and round up to full gallons. If rooms share the same color, buying in 5-gallon buckets (when available) is more economical than multiple 1-gallon cans, and ensures consistent color from a single batch.
