Landscaping Material Estimating: A Contractor's Guide

One of the most common and costly mistakes in landscaping is ordering the wrong amount of material. Whether you are installing a gravel driveway, refreshing mulched garden beds, or spreading topsoil for a lawn renovation, accurate material estimation saves money, eliminates waste, and keeps your project on schedule. These calculators are built using the same formulas professional landscapers and contractors use every day.

Understanding Cubic Yards in Landscaping

Most bulk landscape materials — gravel, mulch, topsoil, sand, and compost — are sold by the cubic yard. One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet, or a cube that is 3 feet wide, 3 feet tall, and 3 feet deep. The formula for any rectangular area is straightforward:

Cubic Yards = (Length × Width × Depth in feet) ÷ 27

Always convert depth to feet before calculating. For example, a 4-inch depth of gravel = 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet. A 3-inch depth of mulch = 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 feet.

Common Landscape Material Depths

Choosing the right depth for each material ensures proper function and appearance:

  • Gravel (driveway) — 4 inches minimum; 6 inches for heavy-duty driveways
  • Gravel (pathway) — 2–3 inches for foot-traffic paths
  • Gravel (drainage/French drain) — 6–12 inches depending on drainage needs
  • Mulch (garden beds) — 2–4 inches; 3 inches is the ideal balance of weed suppression and moisture retention
  • Mulch (tree rings) — 3–4 inches, keeping mulch 6 inches away from the trunk to prevent rot
  • Topsoil (lawn renovation) — 2–4 inches of screened topsoil for seeding or sodding
  • Topsoil (raised beds) — 8–12 inches of amended soil for vegetable gardening
  • Sand (paver base) — 1 inch of bedding sand beneath pavers

Bulk Delivery vs Bags: Which is Right for Your Project?

Bulk delivery by truck is almost always more economical per cubic yard but requires a minimum order (typically 1 cubic yard) and truck access to your site. Bagged materials cost more per cubic yard but are convenient for small areas, hard-to-reach spots, or when you need to bring material upstairs or through a gate.

  • Under 0.5 cubic yards — Bags are usually more practical and similar in cost to delivery minimums and fees
  • 0.5–2 cubic yards — Both options are feasible; compare delivered price vs bag cost
  • Over 2 cubic yards — Bulk delivery is almost always the better value

Compaction and Settling: Always Order Extra

Both gravel and mulch settle and compact after installation. Gravel compacts significantly when driven over or compacted with a plate compactor — expect 10–20% compaction for a new gravel driveway. Mulch settles by 20–25% over its first season as it decomposes. Always order 10–15% extra material to account for settling and any areas where depth varies.

Weight Considerations for Bulk Orders

Landscape materials are heavy, and understanding their weight is important for delivery logistics and planning:

  • Gravel and crushed stone — approximately 1.4–1.5 tons per cubic yard (2,800–3,000 lbs)
  • Pea gravel — approximately 1.3 tons per cubic yard (2,600 lbs)
  • Decomposed granite — approximately 1.2 tons per cubic yard (2,400 lbs)
  • Topsoil (dry) — approximately 1.0 ton per cubic yard (2,000 lbs)
  • Mulch (dry wood chip) — approximately 400–500 lbs per cubic yard (much lighter than stone)

Consider truck weight limits when scheduling deliveries and verify that the delivery vehicle can access your site without damaging lawn, pavers, or underground utilities.

Ordering Tips for Landscaping Materials

  • Always call ahead to confirm material availability, especially for specialty products like decomposed granite or specific gravel sizes
  • Ask about moisture content — wet materials weigh significantly more and may settle differently
  • Request a sample or look at the product in person when color or texture matters for aesthetics
  • Plan your delivery for a day when you can spread the material immediately — piles left in driveways can damage surfaces
  • Keep a small surplus on hand for future top-dressing and repairs
