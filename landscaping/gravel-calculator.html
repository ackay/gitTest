How to Calculate Gravel for Any Project

Whether you're installing a gravel driveway, creating a walking path, improving drainage, or adding decorative stone to a garden bed, calculating the right amount of gravel prevents costly mistakes. Gravel is sold by the cubic yard (volume) or by the ton (weight), and knowing both helps you compare quotes and place accurate orders.

The Gravel Volume Formula

For any rectangular area:

Cubic Yards = (Length × Width × Depth in feet) ÷ 27

Convert depth from inches to feet by dividing by 12. For example: a 4-inch depth = 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet. Then multiply all three dimensions together and divide by 27.

Types of Gravel and Their Best Uses

Choosing the right gravel type makes a significant difference in durability, drainage, and appearance:

  • Standard Gravel (1/2"–1" crushed) — General-purpose gravel for driveways, paths, and fill. Angular particles interlock under compaction for a stable surface. Approximately 1.4 tons per cubic yard.
  • Pea Gravel (3/8" round smooth) — Small, rounded, and attractive. Commonly used in play areas, garden beds, and decorative borders. Does not compact as firmly as crushed stone. Approximately 1.3 tons per cubic yard.
  • Crushed Stone (#57 or #411) — Angular crushed stone in various sizes. #57 stone (1/2"–1") is ideal for drainage, driveway base, and concrete aggregate. Compacts firmly. Approximately 1.5 tons per cubic yard.
  • Decomposed Granite (DG) — Finely crushed granite that compacts into a firm, sand-like surface. Popular for pathways, patios, and drought-tolerant landscaping in the western US. Approximately 1.2 tons per cubic yard.

Recommended Depths by Application

  • Driveway (passenger cars) — 4 inches minimum of compacted base; 2 additional inches of top dressing for a total of 6 inches
  • Driveway (heavy vehicles) — 6-inch base plus 2-inch top for a total of 8 inches
  • Walking paths and garden paths — 2–3 inches of gravel over a compacted base
  • French drain / drainage trench — 6–12 inches of clean crushed stone around the perforated pipe
  • Decorative landscape areas — 1.5–2 inches of pea gravel or decorative stone
  • Playground surface — 6–9 inches of pea gravel or engineered wood fiber for fall protection

Compaction and Settling

All gravel compacts and settles after installation, especially under vehicle traffic. For driveways, allow for 10–15% compaction when ordering — a 4-inch specified depth often requires ordering 4.5 inches worth of material. Use a plate compactor or vibratory roller after spreading gravel to achieve maximum compaction and density before use.

Delivery Options and Planning

Bulk gravel is most commonly delivered by dump truck in 10–15 ton loads (approximately 7–10 cubic yards per load). Some suppliers offer smaller "mini dump" deliveries. Have a plan for where the load will be dumped — gravel should be placed as close to its final location as possible, as moving it by wheelbarrow is labor-intensive. Mark utility lines before digging or distributing heavy loads. Always verify that the delivery vehicle can access your site without damaging underground utilities, irrigation systems, or paved surfaces.

Using Landscape Fabric Under Gravel

Installing woven geotextile landscape fabric beneath gravel helps suppress weeds and prevents gravel from mixing into the soil below. Use commercial-grade fabric (not household weed cloth), overlap seams by 6–12 inches, and secure with landscape staples every 2–3 feet. Avoid non-woven fabric under driveways — it restricts drainage. Permeable landscape fabric is the correct choice for most gravel applications.

The weight per cubic yard depends on the gravel type. Standard gravel: ~1.4 tons/cu yd (2,800 lbs). Crushed stone: ~1.5 tons/cu yd (3,000 lbs). Pea gravel: ~1.3 tons/cu yd (2,600 lbs). Decomposed granite: ~1.2 tons/cu yd (2,400 lbs). These figures are for dry material — wet gravel can weigh 10–15% more. Always ask your supplier for the actual weight of the specific material you're ordering.
Crushed stone or crusher run is the best choice for driveways. The angular edges of crushed stone interlock when compacted, creating a stable surface that doesn't shift under vehicle tires. For the base layer, use 1.5-inch or 2-inch crushed stone. For the surface layer, use 3/4-inch crushed stone or 3/4-inch clean gravel. Pea gravel is not recommended for driveways as it shifts easily and can migrate onto lawns. Decomposed granite works well in dry climates but erodes in heavy rain.
A well-built gravel driveway should have at least 6 inches of total depth — typically a 4-inch compacted base layer of larger aggregate and a 2-inch top layer of smaller decorative gravel. For driveways that will support heavy trucks, RVs, or machinery, use 6–8 inches of base aggregate for a total depth of 8–10 inches. Single-layer gravel should be a minimum of 4 inches deep. Always compact each layer with a plate compactor for maximum stability.
One cubic yard of gravel typically weighs between 2,400 and 3,000 pounds (1.2 to 1.5 tons), depending on the type. Standard gravel: ~2,800 lbs. Crushed stone: ~3,000 lbs. Pea gravel: ~2,600 lbs. Decomposed granite: ~2,400 lbs. Wet gravel weighs more.
